As a parent, kids can motivate you like no other. Here's how a Spongebob episode (The Bully) led to Marvel Cosmic Invasion.

A few weeks ago my two kids watched an episode of Spongebob Squarepants, the one where a new classmate in Sponge's boating school threatens to kick his butt. They found it hilarious enough that my youngest now exclaims "I like kicking butts!" when asked about some of her favorite things to do. "So you like kicking butts, do you?" Of course I showed her a couple beat-em-up style video games, and since my wife's a Marvel fan (hey, I am too, just not as much of one), we settled on Marvel Cosmic Invasion.

Thanks to the power of Fable 5, making a port shaped after JohnnyOnFlame's TNMT:SR was easy. Ok, ok, maybe not *that* easy. I mean, it ran. On my AYN Thor. But we all know that's not good enough. First, which depot do we use? MCI has a linux depot, so the natural inclinatoin is to go with that. Except, the linux depot uses SDL3, and Windows uses SDL2. Problem solved. Next, what's the renderer? Vulkan. That's fine for higher power devices, but OpenGLES is the norm with ports and if I'm going to learn something, I need to *not* be satisfied here.

The first step was forcing FNA3D to use OpenGL + GLES3 instead of MCI's default Vulkan preference. The launchscript enforces this now, but the earliest wall was a black screen hang on every handheld I tested. ParisEngine preloads assets on worker threads that create GL resources; FNA3D queues those to the main thread, but the main thread was gated behind `Present` *and* held locks the workers were waiting on. Classic standoff. Johnny's FNA3D patches for TMNT:SR was the right direction, but MCI differs in that the async split work was needed across both `ParisEngine.dll` and `Game.exe`.

Next, optimization. Johnny's TMNT patch compresses textures, and MCI is full of 4k textures (because the game was designed for modern consoles played on 4k displays). I took things a step further than TMNT and went with *R8 texture encoding* blended with *astc* for static textures. The encoding is classified off the animation data, where the patch can rely on the engne's real "is this indexed" flag and preserve palette swaps.

At this point, Cosmic Invasion was booting and looking good on my RG40xxh, but it wasn't smooth enough. It played okay for a while, but after some playtime the game locked up. Debugging found that the engine pre-creates one live FMOD instance across all four banks and never releases them. FMOD pins sample data while an instance exists, so the resident audio set only grows. The whole SFX bank ends up stuck in unevictable heap, which on weak hardware leads to trashing and out-of-memory.

I attempted a patch which crashed at launch: `klass' not met`; turns out adding fields to the base sound class shifted `Game.exe` subclass's AOT field offsets. Oops, both assemblies (`ParisEngine.dll` and `Game.exe`) need to be AOT'd *together*. The real solution was to reclaim a non-music sound's FMOD instance after a tunable idle grace period and lazily recreate it on the next play. This bounds the resident set to whatever has been recently used, reducing memory by a large margin.

After MCI quit having crashes after extended playtime, it was time to buy back that sweet headroom that TMNT:SR has. Headroom is a bit of extra leeway to account for periodic spikes in CPU and memory during specific gameplay sequences. Without headroom, games can slow to a crawl as things take longer to process through the pipeline. To get headroom, I re-encoded the font atlases from R8 to ASTC 6x6 (same dimensions, no renderer change) which put headroom at around 140MB on the H700 (up from 45MB).

Now I have rocknix installed on all of my devices for multiple reasons: I enjoy rocknix, it gets OTA updates and fast bug fixes, I have more control as a power user, and I haven't kept up with other custom firmwares. I'm not bothered if it turns out my work isn't perfect everywhere, since accounting for every custom firmware under the sun is tantamount to a full time job I don't have time for. Instead, I rely on issues being reported to my github or to me directly on Discord. The day after I published MCI, I received a report of no audio on muOS. So for the first time in a while I flashed the cfw to a spare sd card to debug.

Cosmic Invasion uses FMOD for audio, diverging from TMNT:SR. FMOD picks its device by enumerating ALSA namehints. muOS routes `default` to PipeWire, but hides it from enumeration while PipeWire holds the codec exclusively; FMOD sees nothing playable.
