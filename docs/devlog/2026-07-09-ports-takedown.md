I sent in a request the other day for PortMaster to remove several of my ports from their repository. I've been maintaining them to the best of my ability here on RHH-Ports, and I want users to get (in my opinion) the best experience with the latest working data. Since I parted ways with PortMaster last year, only a few of the community developers over there have been willing and able to mirror some of my maintenance work. For a majority of ports that now existed in two places, there was a split where my maintenance efforts were unseen and unheard. For example, when DELTARUNE'S Chapter 5 released, I was able to get it working and respond to several issues with it on the same day. I fixed a video crash, found some performance patches I could make, and even played through the entire chapter myself.

I shared what I'd found with a PortMaster developer since plenty of people still use that route for downloading and using ports. However I soon found that my maintenance efforts, though adopted, didn't carry as much as I'd hoped. PortMaster has unfortunately recently been forced to adopt a policy of "nothing made outside of PortMaster is mentioned, referred, or supported" thanks to several grey area (if they can even be called that) ports floating around, crafted outside of PortMaster, that rely on either stolen source code, pirated game data, or illegal ports of games in the first place.

To this end, I've removed my wrapper for Hit and Run. Even though it pointed to a different location to actually get the compiled binary to use the port, it was still in a sense providing support. I like to think I'm not the same as shadier characters, and while I do value freedoms to do as I please and not have my efficiency weakened by external systems, hosting that port in hopes of sending a "do better" message and providing visibility was not my wisest decision.

Back to my takedown request: since PortMaster's policy doesn't allow them to refer users to me for my continued maintenance efforts of my own work, I judged that the best outcome for both parties, for the moment, was to request that all ports where I was the sole author be removed from PortMaster. I do maintain my work, I stand behind it, and I certainly don't want PortMaster to give the impression that I've abandoned my work.

PortMaster does appear to recognize that the ports I've authored and helped push forward are valuable, so they do want them hosted. If they reupload, but without my name attached (or minimized), then it means they are truly "PortMaster supported"; their policy would be intact. I can't agree with stripping the history of a port though, and I intend to continue my own path here where I've established my comfortable workflow for a year now.

End of the day I'm a craftsman, not a salesman. The most helpful thing I could receive is visibility, spread by word of mouth (text, in this case). I do this hobby for my kids and the people in front of me, first and foremost, but it's human nature for people to want their efforts and works shared. Everyone wants to be valued.
